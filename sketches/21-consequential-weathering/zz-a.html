    consequential weathering / earthly atmospheres

    When we ask ‘Who has the right to control and change the weather?’, we should also ask ‘Who is already interfering with, and in, the weather at large scales?’

    Can we really achieve and talk about a weather commons, if all systems it is entangled with are extracted and manipulated across scales, and if weather modification itself depends on geological extraction?

    Schwarze Pumpe

    Schwarze Pumpe (the black pump) is a lignite-fired power station in Oberlausitz, Brandenburg, in eastern Germany. Seen from above, the clouds rising from its cooling towers stretch out over the landscape.

    A · II · Extraction modifying the weather

    A cloud factory?

    What can be seen in the satellite imagery is the warm, moist air released by the cooling towers. As it rises and cools, its water vapour condenses into monumental clouds. With shifting local climates, the cooling towers of Schwarze Pumpe quickly became the source of a local metereological myth or conspiracy, spreading on social media. The term 'cloud factory' is one of the ways the power station is described in a posts on Instagram. (include post in small image). Among the wider local public, people began to blame the power station for ongoing rain events and shifts in weather patterns.

    A · III · Extraction modifying the weather

    Weather Machines

    What weight does the myth of the weather altering lignite power plant hold when we are thinking of a weather commons? Is there a truth to the story of the power plant running on geological fuel to create rain? Recently, much more research has emerged confirming that coal-fired power plants, especially lignite ones, do affect weather patterns. One way is through ultrafine particles (UFP), tiny particles in the exhaust, especially from newer filter systems. Schwarze Pumpe has been renovated. Even the German weather service (DWD) has written about how the region’s power stations affect the weather.
